## Category Underwriting

- **Timing:** Rapid workload growth creates opportunity around latency, throughput, memory efficiency, and cost per token, but benchmark comparability and software integration are critical.

- **Primary proof point:** Production workload advantage that survives software, system, and integration overhead.
- **Commercial proof point:** Referenceable deployment, repeat order, or awarded design win with an economic path to scale.
- **Financing risk:** Semiconductor milestones often require capital before revenue evidence; model downside dilution explicitly.
- **Exit test:** Identify the strategic roadmap gap and likely buyer before underwriting M&A as a base case.

### FuriosaAI

**Summary:** FuriosaAI is one of two Korean inference-silicon companies now moving from national-champion status to international deployment. Its core investment case is a power-envelope-constrained accelerator that can be racked in existing colocation facilities without liquid cooling. The underwriting focus should be European and Korean deployment conversion, sustained supply of HBM, and whether revenue can scale before better-capitalized competitors reach the same power efficiency.

**Product / architecture:** Tensor contraction inference accelerators for datacenter LLM serving.

**Differentiation:** Stated 512 dense FP8 TFLOPS within an air-coolable power envelope, which targets deployment in existing colocation facilities rather than purpose-built AI datacenters.

**Commercial status:** early_revenue; Began fielding RNGD at an Equinix datacenter in Lisbon in 2026 and established a Portuguese subsidiary. Reported revenue remains small; Korean press placed 2025 revenue at 5.7bn won.

**VC view:** watchlist at growth stage fit. Why now: European deployment and Korean state backing arrive at the same moment that inference capacity is the binding constraint in AI infrastructure.

**Scorecard:** market size 5/5, timing 5/5, technical differentiation 4/5, founder quality 4/5, customer pull 3/5, business model quality 3/5, capital efficiency 3/5, competitive intensity 2/5, exit potential 4/5, strategic scarcity 4/5

**Potential acquirers:** Samsung, SK hynix, Korean industrial groups

**Key diligence:** What is contracted versus deployed capacity at Equinix Lisbon and elsewhere?; How is HBM supply secured, and at what cost position?; What is the serving-software gap against CUDA-based alternatives?

## Diligence Questions

- What system bottleneck is measured, and under which production workload?
- Which claims are independently verified versus company-reported?
- What are the architecture, node, packaging, memory, and interconnect dependencies?
- How mature are the compiler, runtime, frameworks, debugging, and observability tools?
- What changes are required in the customer's application or infrastructure?
- What are tapeout, yield, qualification, supply-chain, and second-source risks?
- What performance, power, cost, utilization, and reliability metrics matter to buyers?
- What incumbent roadmap or internal customer alternative could erase the advantage?
