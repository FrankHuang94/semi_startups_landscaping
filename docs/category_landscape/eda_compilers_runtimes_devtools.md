## Category Underwriting

- **Timing:** Software can offer better capital efficiency and recurring revenue, but distribution, incumbent bundling, open source, and narrow initial markets require careful diligence.

- **Primary proof point:** Production workload advantage that survives software, system, and integration overhead.
- **Commercial proof point:** Referenceable deployment, repeat order, or awarded design win with an economic path to scale.
- **Financing risk:** Semiconductor milestones often require capital before revenue evidence; model downside dilution explicitly.
- **Exit test:** Identify the strategic roadmap gap and likely buyer before underwriting M&A as a base case.

### ChipAgents

**Summary:** ChipAgents is the best-capitalized of the agentic EDA entrants, with a strategic register that includes memory, mobile, and communications vendors. Its core investment case is that verification labour, not tool licences, is the binding constraint in chip design. The underwriting focus should be measured productivity on production RTL rather than demonstration flows, and whether incumbents close the gap with their own agent layers.

**Product / architecture:** Agentic AI environment for RTL design, debug, and verification.

**Differentiation:** Agents operate across specification, RTL generation, verification, and debug rather than a single assistive step.

**Commercial status:** early_revenue; Strategic investment from Micron, MediaTek, and Ericsson is an adoption signal. Named production deployments and seat counts are not disclosed.

**VC view:** watchlist at early_stage stage fit. Why now: Custom silicon demand is outrunning available design and verification engineers, and coding models have become good enough to attack the verification loop.

**Scorecard:** market size 4/5, timing 5/5, technical differentiation 3/5, founder quality 4/5, customer pull 3/5, business model quality 4/5, capital efficiency 4/5, competitive intensity 2/5, exit potential 4/5, strategic scarcity 3/5

## Diligence Questions

- What system bottleneck is measured, and under which production workload?
- Which claims are independently verified versus company-reported?
- What are the architecture, node, packaging, memory, and interconnect dependencies?
- How mature are the compiler, runtime, frameworks, debugging, and observability tools?
- What changes are required in the customer's application or infrastructure?
- What are tapeout, yield, qualification, supply-chain, and second-source risks?
- What performance, power, cost, utilization, and reliability metrics matter to buyers?
- What incumbent roadmap or internal customer alternative could erase the advantage?
